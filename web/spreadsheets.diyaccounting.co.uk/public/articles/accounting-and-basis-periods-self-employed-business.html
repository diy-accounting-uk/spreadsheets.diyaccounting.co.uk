Accounting Periods And Basis Periods For Self Employed Business

Basis periods no longer apply to a sole trader. From the 2024 to 2025 tax year you are taxed on the profits that arise in the tax year, which runs from 6 April to 5 April. You can still choose your own accounting date, such as 31 December. The profits of your accounting periods are then added together or apportioned to find the profit for each tax year.

Accounts are required each year for tax and financial control purposes with preset dates by which those accounting records must be submitted and penalties for failure to deliver on time.

Before 2024 a self employed business that chose a different accounting date was taxed on a basis period, which could overlap two tax years. That reform is complete. The 2023 to 2024 tax year was the transition year. The tax year basis applies from 2024 to 2025.

Self employed business in the UK is required to produce a set of financial accounts for a 12 month trading period. The format of the self employed accounts is the personal decision of the proprietor and can be a full set of annual accounts including profit and loss account and balance sheet including using control accounts and cash and bank records and the self assessment tax return.

An appropriate accounting system for many self employed business would not be to prepare a full set of annual accounts but instead to prepare a simple income and expenditure account. Preparing an income and expenditure account allows a much simpler accounting or bookkeeping system where simple accounting software can be used.

The objective of any bookkeeping software is being able to maintain accurate financial records and produce the accounting records and totals required to complete the inland revenue self employment tax return each year. Financial control is very important and the bookkeeping software should also produce regular financial statements showing the profit and loss of the business throughout the accounting trading periods.

The financial tax year varies depending upon which country business is conducted. In the US accounts are prepared during an accounting period from 1 January to 31 December each year. In the UK the standard financial year adopted by the inland revenue is from 6 April each year to the 5 April the following year.

In the UK tax rules are set for each tax year. A small business that adopts the standard tax year prepares its accounts under a single set of tax rules and prepares the self assessment tax return from the same figures. A different accounting date means the profits of more than one accounting period go into one tax year, and more than one set of tax rules might apply to the calculation.

A sole trader files the online Self Assessment tax return by 31 January after the end of the tax year and pays the tax due by the same date. For the 2025 to 2026 tax year that is 31 January 2027. A paper return is due earlier: 31 October 2026.

A year end of 5 April makes the accounting period and the tax year the same. If the accounting date falls from 31 March to 4 April, the profits for the days after the accounting date, up to and including 5 April, are treated as arising in the following tax year.

A late Self Assessment return costs an initial £100 penalty. After 3 months there are daily penalties of £10 a day, up to £900. After 6 months there is a further penalty of 5% of the tax due or £300, whichever is greater, and the same again after 12 months. HMRC also charges interest on tax paid late.

If your qualifying income is over £50,000, Making Tax Digital for Income Tax has applied to you since 6 April 2026. It applies from 6 April 2027 if your qualifying income for 2025 to 2026 is over £30,000.
