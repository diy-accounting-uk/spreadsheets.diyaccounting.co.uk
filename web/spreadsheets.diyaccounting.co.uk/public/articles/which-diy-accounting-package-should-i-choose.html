Which DIY Accounting package should I choose?

Choose the package that matches how your business is set up. A sole trader without VAT or staff uses Basic Sole Trader. A self employed person with VAT or staff uses Self Employed. A taxi or private hire driver uses Taxi Driver. A limited company uses Company Accounts. An employer who only needs payslips uses Payslip.

Sole traders

The Basic Sole Trader package is for sole traders who are not VAT registered and have no employees. The Self Employed package is for anyone self employed, VAT registered or not, with or without employees. It also holds the VAT returns, the bank reconciliation and payslips.

The VAT line decides most choices. You must register for VAT if your taxable turnover for the last 12 months goes over £90,000.[1] You can also choose to register below that figure.[2] Once you are registered, Self Employed is the package that holds your VAT returns.

Taxi and private hire drivers

The Taxi Driver package is for self-employed taxi drivers, private hire drivers, van drivers and couriers. It compares the mileage allowance with your actual vehicle costs and selects the most tax-efficient method for your expenses claim.

Limited companies

The Company Accounts package uses double-entry bookkeeping. It produces VAT returns, a profit and loss account, a balance sheet, year-end accounts and the corporation tax figures for the CT600. The package comes in a version for each company year-end month.

A company files its return with commercial software. HMRC's online service for company accounts and the Company Tax Return closed on 31 March 2026.[3] Read more in tax accounting software and the company tax return.

Employers

The Payslip 05 package prints payslips for 5 or 10 staff, for use beside a separate tax calculator such as HMRC's Basic PAYE Tools. The Self Employed and Company packages include their own payslip workbooks.

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax applies to qualifying income over £50,000 from 6 April 2026, over £30,000 from 6 April 2027 and over £20,000 from 6 April 2028.[4] The sole trader packages keep the records and total the figures. They do not send an update to HMRC. Read what a cumulative quarterly update is before you plan around it.
