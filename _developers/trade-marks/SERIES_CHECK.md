# Series check: DIY ACCOUNTING SUBMIT and DIY ACCOUNTING SPREADSHEETS

## Answer

**No. The two marks do not qualify as a series.** File them as two separate applications.

## The test

Section 41(2) of the Trade Marks Act 1994, quoted in full from
[legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1994/26/section/41):

> A series of trade marks means a number of trade marks which resemble each other as to their
> material particulars and differ only as to matters of a non-distinctive character not
> substantially affecting the identity of the trade mark.

The IPO's own Manual of trade marks practice, examination guide
(`https://www.gov.uk/guidance/trade-marks-manual/the-examination-guide`), applies this by asking
whether a single search covers every mark in the set, and treats a difference that changes the
mark's identity — its example is a font change substantial enough to do this — as taking the marks
outside the series rule.

## Applying it

The two marks:

- DIY ACCOUNTING SUBMIT
- DIY ACCOUNTING SPREADSHEETS

share "DIY ACCOUNTING" and differ in one word: SUBMIT against SPREADSHEETS. That word is not a
non-distinctive matter like a font, a colour, punctuation, or a plural. It is the part of each mark
that says what the product is — one names a filing service, the other names a set of downloadable
workbooks. Two different products, described in different words, each carrying its own meaning: the
difference is exactly the kind that affects the identity of the mark, which is what section 41(2)
rules out.

The IPO's practical test points the same way: clearing SUBMIT and clearing SPREADSHEETS are two
different searches, against different prior marks and different specifications (a filing service
sits more naturally against class 42 SaaS and class 35 tax-return services; a spreadsheet product
sits more naturally against class 9 downloadable software). A series application is meant for one
search covering near-identical marks, not two.

## Fee consequence

Since the marks are not a series, `H-LU-4` files them as two separate Right Start applications, not
one series application. `GOODS_AND_SERVICES.md` in this pack prices that route: £370 each at the
fees in force from 1 April 2026, £740 for both, with no series fee saving.

Had the rule held, the saving would have been one mark's full fee minus a series fee: one Right
Start application (£370 in three classes) rather than two (£740), minus the £60 online series fee
for the second mark — a saving of about £310. That saving is not available here.
